About

Where I’m coming from

I grew up in Maple Valley, Washington and went through Running Start, which meant leaving high school with an associate degree in computer science from Green River College — 90 credits at a 3.77 GPA — already finished. I’m now a junior at Washington State University studying computer science with a minor in mathematics, graduating December 2027, holding a 3.88 GPA including a 4.0 semester this past spring.

Most of what I’ve built started as a course assignment I wasn’t finished with.

The clearest example is the guessing-game API. It began as a recursive binary-tree project in an intro course, where the tree only had to survive one run on one machine. Putting it on the web broke that assumption, so I rebuilt the traversal as an iterative, stateless walk, moved the tree into PostgreSQL so every visitor shares and extends the same one, and containerized the service. Finding out exactly where the original design stopped holding taught me more than the assignment did.

That same question — what breaks when one becomes many — is what made last summer click. On SanMar’s software operations team in Issaquah I wasn’t writing application code; I was working out why 68 groups of Group Policy settings couldn’t follow a fleet of devices into modern management, then scripting the migration into Intune in PowerShell. Policy that works on one machine and policy that holds across thousands of endpoints are different problems, and the second one is the interesting one.

This fall I’m taking Software Engineering I, Machine Learning, Programming Language Design and Automata, and shipping projects alongside them. I’m looking for a Summer 2027 software engineering internship, ideally back in the Puget Sound area.
